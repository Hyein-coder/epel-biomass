### Arm A — Scale and biomass supply capacity

### ¶3. Scale lowers conversion cost, but biomass supply pushes back

- **Role:** frames §3.1 Steps 1–3.
- **Line of argument:**
  1. Conversion cost falls with plant size, so TEAs commonly scale capital with a 0.6 exponent and keep feedstock price flat [cite needed]. Under those two assumptions the cheapest plant is always the largest.
  2. Both assumptions break at large scale. Biomass is dispersed and finite: as demand outgrows the local low-cost resource, the marginal feedstock costs more, so feedstock price follows a rising supply curve rather than a constant [8, cite needed for supply curves, e.g. Galik]. On the equipment side, the pyrolyzer, compressors, gas turbines and expanders reach single-train limits and must be numbered up into parallel trains, which removes further economies of scale [cite needed].
  3. Korea frames the stakes: forests cover 63% of the land, yet the domestic resource is bounded relative to national jet-fuel demand.
- **Gap:** few SAF TEAs model the supply-curve price and the single-train limits together, so the minimum efficient size of a pyrolysis SAF plant is not known.
- **Kept from old intro:** the scale-up paradox paragraph (old p3–4, ref 8), reframed from haulage cost to feedstock price, since haulage is excluded in §3.1 and only noted there.

### Arm B — Hydrogen economy and sustainability

### ¶4. Bio-oil upgrading ties SAF emissions to the hydrogen source

- **Role:** frames why the grey-H₂ base misses CORSIA (§3.2 Step 2).
- **Kept:** hydrotreating needs H₂ above one-third of the dry oil feed by mass [4]; today this H₂ comes from SMR at ~4–8 kg CO₂e/kg H₂, even with CCS [9]. Reliance on grey H₂ erodes the GHG benefit of biogenic carbon and keeps the fuel tied to fossil gas markets.
- **Add:** one sentence that this makes the hydrogen source, not the biomass, the main emission and cost risk of pyrolysis SAF. §3.3 confirms it (grey-H₂ price is the dominant SHAP driver).

### ¶5. Two levers with different cost structures: green ammonia and process CCS

- **Role:** sets up the 6 × 5 grid (§3.2 Step 1) and the crossover (§3.3).
- **Line of argument:**
  1. Green ammonia: a dense, tradable H₂ carrier that can be cracked on site [10, 11]. Its place in the emerging hydrogen economy, including import-based supply for countries such as Korea [cite needed], makes it the natural route for replacing grey H₂. Its cost scales with the share of H₂ it displaces.
  2. Post-combustion CCS on the plant's own flue gas: it removes process CO₂ directly, without changing the H₂ supply. Its cost depends on the capture fraction.
  3. The two levers therefore trade cost for emissions differently, and their relative merit depends on grey-H₂ and NH₃ prices, which are themselves uncertain.
- **Gap:** no study maps the joint NH₃-share × CCS-portion space for pyrolysis SAF to find the cheapest CORSIA-compliant design, its abatement cost beyond compliance, and the hydrogen-price conditions under which green NH₃ would beat CCS.
- **Change from old intro:** drop SMR-with-CCS as a separate pathway and drop PEM (removed from the manuscript on 2026-09-30). Hydrogen options are grey H₂ (SMR) and green-NH₃-cracked H₂ only.

### ¶6. Objective and contributions

- **Role:** joins Arms A and B; one contribution per Results section.
- **Draft:** This study combines TEA and LCA of a forest-residue fast-pyrolysis SAF process to answer two questions in sequence: how large the plant should be once feedstock supply is priced in, and, at that size, how the plant should source hydrogen and capture carbon to meet CORSIA at the lowest cost.
  1. Scale (§3.1): finds the minimum-MJSP scale by combining single-train equipment limits with three biomass price models (flat, FR–Log-Linear, Fixed Elasticity).
  2. Trade-off (§3.2): at that scale, maps 30 green-NH₃-share × CCS-portion configurations in cost–emission space, identifies the cheapest compliant configuration, and costs further abatement.
  3. Robustness (§3.3): tests the ranking against price uncertainty (tornado, SHAP) and locates the grey-H₂ price at which green NH₃ overtakes CCS-only.
