# 4. Conclusion

1. **Scale matters on two fronts — displacing conventional jet fuel and respecting feedstock supply.**
Meaningful substitution of fossil jet needs volume, so the plant must scale up; but domestic forest-residue supply is finite, so scale cannot be chosen on economies alone. Scale economies and a rising biomass supply curve pull opposite ways and cross at 0.414 MMt/yr SAF, the minimum-MJSP size. Scaling from 0.041 to 1.243 MMt/yr cuts MJSP by ~60% (\$1.64 → \$0.66/L), but about 90% of that is captured by 0.414 MMt/yr — above it, single-train limits push the cost exponent from ~0.8 to ~1.0 and feedstock escalation erases further gains.

2. **The grey-hydrogen base case fails CORSIA; both levers can fix it.**
Base intensity 100.8 g CO₂e/MJ sits above the 75.93 g threshold. Across the green-NH₃ × CCS space the design spans \$0.79/L at 100.8 g CO₂e/MJ to \$3.09/L at 21.9 g CO₂e/MJ — a 4.6-fold emissions cut for a 3.9-fold price rise.

3. **CCS is the cheaper decarbonization lever; compliance is cheapest with CCS first.**
Per gram removed, CCS beats green NH₃. The lowest-cost compliant design is CCS 60%; green NH₃ is added last, only beyond compliance.

4. **Green ammonia behaves linearly and predictably.**
Each +10% NH₃ share cuts 3.81 g CO₂e/MJ and adds \$0.185/L at every CCS level — a constant \$0.049/L per g CO₂e/MJ. Green NH₃ out-competes CCS only above a grey-H₂ price crossover.

5. **Hydrogen price governs the economics regardless of compliance route.**
Grey-H₂ price is the dominant MJSP driver in both routes (SHAP \$0.310/L at CCS 60%, \$0.274/L at green NH₃ 10% + CCS 50%; about 3× the next driver). Tornado MJSP ranges are \$0.55–1.84/L for the grey base and \$1.91–4.26/L for full NH₃ with CCS 100%.
