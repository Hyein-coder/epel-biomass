# 4. Conclusion

1. **Scale matters on two fronts — displacing conventional jet fuel and respecting feedstock supply.**  
Meaningful substitution of fossil jet needs volume, so the plant must scale up; but domestic forest-residue supply is finite, so scale cannot be chosen on economies alone. Scale economies and a rising biomass supply curve pull opposite ways and cross at 0.41 MMt/yr SAF, the minimum-MJSP size. Scaling from 0.04 to 1.24 MMt/yr cuts MJSP by approximately 60% below $1/L threshold, but about 90% of that is captured by 0.41 MMt/yr — above it, single-train limits push the cost exponent from ~0.8 to ~1.0 and feedstock escalation erases further gains.
2. **The grey-hydrogen base case fails CORSIA; both levers can fix it.**  
Base intensity 100.8 g CO₂e/MJ sits above the 75.93 g threshold. Across the green-NH₃ × CCS space the design spans to 21.9 g CO₂e/MJ increasing the production cost from $ 0.79/L to $ 3.09/L — a 80% emissions cut for a 4-fold price rise.
3. **CCS is the cheaper decarbonization lever; compliance is cheapest with CCS first.**
Per gram removed, CCS beats green NH₃. The lowest-cost compliant design is CCS 60%; green NH₃ is added last, only beyond compliance.
4. **Green ammonia behaves linearly and predictably since it is opex-driven process.**  
Green NH₃ out-competes CCS above a grey-H₂ price crossover, since grey-H₂ is the dominant MJSP driver in both routes.
