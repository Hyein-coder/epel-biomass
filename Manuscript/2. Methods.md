# 2. Methods

## 2.1 Process design

### 2.1.1 Main process unit

The process is a biomass fast-pyrolysis-to-fuels flowsheet adapted from the NREL/PNNL
design of Jones et al. (2013). The Aspen Plus flowsheet is costed in Aspen Process
Economic Analyzer (APEA); the resulting "Main" block (whole-flowsheet CAPEX/OPEX read
from the APEA Project Summary sheet) is combined with five literature-interpolated
sub-units (CCS, PSA hydrogen recovery, off-gas reformer, hydrotreating catalysts, and
the R-510 hydrocracker). The flowsheet is organized into eight areas: feed handling,
pyrolysis (reactor R-110, area 100), hydrotreating (area 200), product finishing,
hydrogen recovery, hydrocracking (R-510, area 500), off-gas combustion, and the
steam/balance-of-plant island.

Four changes distinguish this design from the Jones et al. (2013) base case:

- **Product slate.** The plant is retuned to maximize sustainable aviation fuel (SAF,
  stream 326SAF) for the South Korean market rather than the gasoline/diesel blendstock
  of the original design. Naphtha-range (316GAS) and diesel-range (336DIES) streams are
  retained as priced co-products.
- **Power co-product.** A gas-turbine power island (ELECGEN) is added so that surplus
  electricity is exported as a co-product, absent from the Jones base case.
- **Hydrogen-supply flexibility.** The grey hydrogen of the original design can be
  replaced, in whole or in part, by green-ammonia-cracked hydrogen, SMR-with-CCS
  hydrogen, or PEM-electrolysis hydrogen (§2.1.2, §2.2.2).
- **Carbon capture.** An amine CCS retrofit is added on the recoverable CO₂ streams
  (§2.1.3).

Base-case feed is 45.5 t/hr of woody biomass; the bio-oil sent to hydrotreating is
33.7 t/hr (≈71 wt% of the biomass). Fresh make-up hydrogen totals 3.3 t/hr
(2.92 t/hr to hydrotreating stream 250H2, 0.37 t/hr to hydrocracking stream 532H2).
Hydrotreating uses ruthenium catalysts in reactors R-202 and R-220 (LHSV 0.5) and a
molybdenum catalyst in R-225 (LHSV 0.22); catalyst charging is costed separately from
the vessels.

**Scale-up and single-train limits.** Capacity is scaled from the base case up to a
30× multiple. Rather than applying a single blanket cost exponent, each major unit
carries an explicit single-train ceiling and is "numbered up" into parallel trains once
that ceiling is reached — the pyrolyzer at 700 MMBtu/hr (10× the base duty of
70 MMBtu/hr), and likewise the gas turbines, compressors, and expanders. As a
consequence the effective CAPEX scaling exponent of the main plant rises from about 0.8
below ≈0.42 MMt/yr SAF to about 1.0 above it, which is what defines the minimum
efficient scale reported in §3.1. This train-level detail is realized inside the
per-scale APEA files, not by a closed-form correlation.

All costs are reported in first-quarter 2022 USD; Jones-era (2011) source costs are
escalated by the cost-index ratio 3230/2250. Financial basis: 8000 operating hours per
year, a 30-year economic life, a 10% internal rate of return, and a 35% tax rate.

### 2.1.2 Ammonia cracker

Green ammonia is used as a hydrogen carrier: imported NH₃ is catalytically cracked to
supply make-up hydrogen. The fraction of make-up hydrogen met by cracked ammonia is set
by the `ammonia_portion` knob, with the remainder left as grey hydrogen. The cracker is
sized to the plant's make-up hydrogen demand (indexed against the 6,570 lb/hr base-case
make-up rate) and costed from a sweep of Aspen/APEA cracker simulations
(`NH3_Cracking_130` series) spanning the same 1–30× scale set as the main plant, with
linear interpolation between simulated scales. Cracker reactors carry a single-train
ceiling of 172 MMBtu/hr. The cracking reaction network (GHSV, kinetics) follows the
source used for the Aspen model (Int. J. Hydrogen Energy, 2024); ammonia conversion and
hydrogen recovery are embedded in that simulation rather than imposed as fixed
constants. A nitrogen-fertilizer stream is credited as an ammonia-side co-product. The
levelized cost of hydrogen (LCOH) from the cracker is benchmarked against grey hydrogen
at 0.68 $/lb H₂.

### 2.1.3 Carbon capture and storage (CCS)

CO₂ capture is a post-combustion amine (MEA) retrofit, cost-interpolated from the
Rao & Rubin (2002) power-plant amine-capture base case (base stream 862,853 lb/hr CO₂;
total CAPEX $297.9 M; regeneration heat 3,315 MMBtu/hr; auxiliary electricity at 14% of
the heat duty, all rebased to 1Q-2022 USD). *(The engine comment labels this "Rao,
2022"; the intended and verified reference is Rao, A. B.; Rubin, E. S. Environ. Sci.
Technol. 2002, 36 (20), 4467–4475.)*

The captured mass is `co2_seq = ccs_portion × (623CO2) + (622X)`: the fraction
`ccs_portion` of the recoverable process CO₂ stream (623CO2) plus the EGR CO₂ stream
(622X), which is always fully captured. Capture-unit CAPEX scales with the captured mass
by the six-tenths rule (exponent 0.6), OPEX by exponent 0.7, and utility draws linearly.
Capture OPEX comprises make-up fuel for any regeneration heat not met by on-site heat,
auxiliary electricity, and optional CO₂ transport-and-storage (T&S; §2.2.3). The capture
portion is swept from 0 to 1.0.

## 2.2 Techno-economic analysis

The economic metric is the minimum jet-fuel selling price (MJSP, $/L SAF), obtained from
a discounted-cash-flow calculation of the SAF price that zeroes net present value over
the 30-year life at a 10% IRR and 35% tax, with straight-line depreciation and zero
salvage. Equivalently, MJSP = (minimum required revenue − co-product sales) / annual SAF
volume. Co-product sales (naphtha, diesel, exported electricity, ammonia-side fertilizer)
are credited on the cost side. Utility prices: electricity 19.05 $/MMBtu (≈0.065 $/kWh),
natural gas 14.38 $/MMBtu.

### 2.2.1 Feedstock price model

Because domestic forest-residue supply is finite, the delivered biomass price is treated
as a function of plant scale rather than a fixed input — a larger plant must draw on
progressively costlier feedstock. Biomass demand is mapped to scale as
demand = scale × 91,014 lb/hr, and three supply-curve models are compared:

- **FR Price (reference).** A constant forest-residue price of 0.048649 $/lb, independent
  of scale — the optimistic floor.
- **FR–Log Linear** (naive Galik supply curve). The forest-residue price holds until
  regional forest-residue supply (783,486 lb/hr) is exhausted, beyond which the delivered
  price rises linearly with demand, price = (supply × 0.04128 + 15107)/10⁶ $/lb, as
  logging residue is drawn in.
- **Fixed Elasticity** (Galik supply curve, supply elasticity 0.3). Follows the
  FR–Log Linear curve until the combined forest-residue-plus-log supply (1,685,782 lb/hr)
  is exhausted, then extrapolates linearly under a fixed supply elasticity of 0.3 (log
  reference price 0.087275 $/lb), giving the steepest scale penalty.

### 2.2.2 Green ammonia and hydrogen supply

Green ammonia is priced at 0.288 $/lb NH₃ (base), swept over 0.1225 $/lb (projected,
future) to 0.4535 $/lb (current). Ammonia-cracked hydrogen substitutes grey hydrogen at
the `ammonia_portion` fraction; the cost-and-emissions optimum used in the analysis is
≈0.655. The grey-hydrogen reference is SMR-with-CCS costed from Katebah (2022)
(0.68 $/lb H₂). A PEM-electrolysis alternative is also modeled (40 kWh/kg H₂, 70%
efficiency, 2000 $/kW electrolyzer from DOE 2024, 0.5 capacity factor for Korean PV).

### 2.2.3 CCS cost

Capture cost follows §2.1.3. CO₂ transport and storage is applied to the sequestered
mass at a base rate of 10 $/t CO₂ (sensitivity range 4–45 $/t CO₂); the T&S cost adder
is linear in the T&S price.

### 2.2.4 Sensitivity analysis

Three complementary sensitivity treatments are run:

- **Tornado.** Eight parameters are perturbed one at a time for three configurations
  (grey-H₂ base, full green ammonia, full CCS) at both the smallest and largest scale:
  biomass price ±50%, grey-H₂ price 1.0–4.0 $/kg, ammonia price 0.1225–0.4535 $/lb,
  CAPEX ±30%, CO₂ T&S 4–45 $/t (CCS only), electricity price ±30%, co-product price
  ±20%, and capacity factor 85%/91%. Price-like parameters are scaled analytically about
  the base run; the rest are re-run with the corresponding knob reset.
- **Pareto front.** With the main plant fixed at the knee-point scale (10×), the
  ammonia portion (0–1) and CCS portion (0–1.0) are gridded into 30 configurations, and
  the non-dominated set is found minimizing both MJSP and WTWa GHG-100 intensity. Each
  point carries an MJSP uncertainty band drawn from the tornado drivers.
- **CO₂ abatement cost.** Computed as ΔMJSP × 10⁶ / (ΔGHG × E), where E = 31.66 MJ/L is
  the volumetric energy of SAF, giving $/t CO₂ avoided relative to the grey-H₂ base.

## 2.3 Life-cycle assessment

### 2.3.1 System boundary and biogenic carbon

The functional unit is 1 MJ of SAF and the boundary is well-to-wake (WTWa), taken as the
sum of well-to-pump (WTP) and pump-to-wake (PTWa, i.e. combustion) stages; the combustion
stage is taken from the Fischer–Tropsch-jet reference. Impacts are reported as GHG-100
(100-year global warming potential) in g CO₂e per MJ SAF, using GREET-derived
inventories.

Biogenic carbon is treated under carbon neutrality: biogenic combustion CO₂ is carried as
a separate species (CO₂_Biogenic) distinct from fossil CO₂ so that the biomass uptake and
its combustion release net to zero, while any biogenic CO₂ diverted to CCS registers as a
negative emission. A time-resolved (dynamic) LCA variant is also available, propagating
each pulse through the Bern impulse-response carbon-climate model (Levasseur, 2010) with a
sequestration trajectory (Lan, 2021); it is used for the dynamic-GWP illustration only,
not for the headline intensities.

### 2.3.2 Allocation

Co-product emissions are handled by allocation, which is used for the central estimate.
Displacement (system-expansion credit) is computed alongside it, and the two bracket the
reported value: the central intensity is the allocation result and the reported spread is
the allocation-to-displacement range. This choice keeps the SAF intensity insensitive to
volatile displacement credits (grid electricity, fertilizer) whose market values swing the
displacement result far more than the allocation result — the allocation basis therefore
gives the more stable and conservative headline number, with displacement retained as the
uncertainty bound. Co-products carried in the allocation are exported electricity and, in
the ammonia configurations, nitrogen fertilizer. Both U.S.-grid and Korea-grid electricity
mixes are evaluated.

### 2.3.3 SAF compliance criterion

Compliance is judged against the CORSIA rule: an eligible SAF must emit at least 10% less
than the conventional-jet baseline on a WTWa GHG-100 basis, i.e. the threshold is
0.9 × (conventional-jet WTWa GHG-100). The conventional baseline is taken from the GREET
inventory. On this basis the grey-H₂ base case (87.6 g CO₂e/MJ) is non-compliant, while
full green ammonia (49.5 g CO₂e/MJ) and 60% CCS (60.3 g CO₂e/MJ) both clear the threshold.
